# KPI Definitions & Financial Interpretation

This document explains the main financial KPIs used in the FP&A Financial Performance Dashboard and how they can be interpreted from a management perspective.

---

## 1. Revenue

### Definition

Revenue represents the total income generated by the company from its business activities before deducting operating costs.

### Formula

Revenue = Total Sales

### Dashboard Result

**Revenue: €4.182M**

### Interpretation

The company generated €4.182M in revenue during the analyzed period.

Revenue should be compared with the budget to determine whether commercial performance is above or below management expectations.

---

## 2. Budget Revenue

### Definition

Budget Revenue represents the revenue target established during the budgeting and planning process.

### Dashboard Result

**Budget Revenue: €4.070M**

### Interpretation

Management expected the company to generate €4.070M in revenue.

Comparing actual revenue with this target helps identify favorable or unfavorable performance deviations.

---

## 3. Revenue Variance

### Definition

Revenue Variance measures the difference between actual revenue and budgeted revenue.

### Formula

Revenue Variance = Actual Revenue - Budget Revenue

### Dashboard Result

Actual Revenue = €4.182M  
Budget Revenue = €4.070M

Revenue Variance ≈ **+€112K**

### Interpretation

The positive variance indicates that actual revenue exceeded the annual budget.

This represents a **favorable variance**, suggesting that the company performed better than initially planned in terms of revenue generation.

---

## 4. Revenue Variance %

### Definition

Revenue Variance % measures the relative difference between actual revenue and budgeted revenue.

### Formula

Revenue Variance % =
(Actual Revenue - Budget Revenue) / Budget Revenue

### Dashboard Result

**+2.73%**

### Interpretation

Actual revenue was approximately **2.73% above budget**.

Overall annual performance is therefore favorable.

However, the monthly analysis shows that performance was not consistent throughout the year.

Several months exceeded budget significantly, while March, June and October recorded negative revenue variances.

This highlights the importance of analyzing both annual and monthly performance rather than relying only on the annual result.

---

## 5. COGS

### Definition

COGS (Cost of Goods Sold) represents the direct costs associated with producing the goods or services sold by the company.

Examples may include:

- Raw materials
- Production costs
- Direct labor
- Manufacturing expenses

### Formula

Gross Profit = Revenue - COGS

### Interpretation

COGS is an important indicator of operational efficiency.

An increase in COGS without a proportional increase in revenue can reduce gross margin and profitability.

FP&A teams therefore monitor COGS against both revenue and budget.

---

## 6. Gross Profit

### Definition

Gross Profit measures the profit generated after deducting the direct cost of producing goods or services.

### Formula

Gross Profit = Revenue - COGS

### Interpretation

A growing Gross Profit generally indicates that the company is generating more value from its core operations.

However, Gross Profit should also be analyzed through Gross Margin to account for changes in revenue levels.

---

## 7. Gross Margin %

### Definition

Gross Margin measures the percentage of revenue remaining after deducting COGS.

### Formula

Gross Margin % =
Gross Profit / Revenue

### Interpretation

A higher Gross Margin generally indicates stronger profitability at the production or service-delivery level.

A declining Gross Margin may indicate:

- Higher production costs
- Supplier price increases
- Pricing pressure
- Product mix deterioration
- Lower operational efficiency

---

## 8. OPEX

### Definition

OPEX (Operating Expenses) represents the operating costs required to run the company that are not directly included in COGS.

Examples include:

- Salaries
- Marketing expenses
- Rent
- IT costs
- Administrative expenses
- Professional services

### Interpretation

The dashboard compares actual OPEX with Budget OPEX for each month.

This allows management to identify periods of overspending or cost savings.

An unfavorable OPEX variance should be investigated to understand whether it results from temporary expenses, structural cost increases or operational inefficiencies.

---

## 9. OPEX Variance

### Definition

OPEX Variance measures the difference between actual operating expenses and budgeted operating expenses.

### Formula

OPEX Variance =
Actual OPEX - Budget OPEX

### Interpretation

For expenses, interpretation is different from revenue.

**Actual OPEX > Budget OPEX**

Usually represents an **unfavorable variance**, because the company spent more than planned.

**Actual OPEX < Budget OPEX**

Usually represents a **favorable variance**, because operating expenses were below budget.

However, lower spending is not automatically positive if it results from delayed investments or activities that could negatively affect future performance.

---

## 10. EBITDA

### Definition

EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortization.

It provides an indication of the profitability generated by the company's core operations.

### Simplified Formula Used in This Project

EBITDA =
Revenue - COGS - OPEX

### Dashboard Result

**EBITDA: €1.449M**

### Interpretation

The company generated approximately €1.449M of EBITDA from €4.182M of revenue.

The positive EBITDA indicates that the company's core operations are profitable before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization.

---

## 11. EBITDA Margin

### Definition

EBITDA Margin measures EBITDA as a percentage of Revenue.

### Formula

EBITDA Margin % =
EBITDA / Revenue

### Dashboard Result

**34.65%**

### Interpretation

The company generated approximately **€0.35 of EBITDA for every €1 of revenue**.

An EBITDA Margin of 34.65% indicates strong operating profitability within this fictional business model.

The monthly dashboard also shows fluctuations in EBITDA Margin.

The margin reaches its lowest level around May before recovering during the second half of the year and reaching its highest level in December.

This trend suggests an improvement in operating profitability toward year-end.

---

# Management Insights

The dashboard highlights several important FP&A insights:

1. **Annual revenue exceeded budget by 2.73%.**

   Overall commercial performance was above management expectations.

2. **Revenue performance varied significantly by month.**

   March, June and October recorded negative revenue variances, while several other months significantly exceeded budget.

3. **EBITDA reached €1.449M with a 34.65% margin.**

   The company maintained strong operating profitability over the analyzed period.

4. **EBITDA Margin improved toward year-end.**

   After declining during the first part of the year, profitability recovered and reached its highest level in December.

5. **OPEX performance should be monitored alongside revenue.**

   Revenue growth alone does not guarantee improved profitability if operating expenses increase faster than sales.

6. **Management should investigate unfavorable monthly variances.**

   Monthly variance analysis can help determine whether deviations are caused by sales performance, pricing, costs, seasonality or operational factors.

---

# FP&A Conclusion

Overall, the company finished the year above its revenue budget while maintaining a strong EBITDA margin.

From an FP&A perspective, management should focus not only on the positive annual result but also on the monthly volatility observed in revenue and operating profitability.

Future analysis could include forecasting, rolling forecasts, scenario analysis and more detailed cost-center or business-unit analysis.
